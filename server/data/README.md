# Server data

## Genre vocabulary

The canonical `genre:` list (TAG-077 to TAG-084, ADR-0023). Read once at start by `app/genre_vocab.py`; the server refuses to start without these two files.

- `genre_discogs.json`: `families` (the kept level-1 names in the fixed order of GEN-037: 9 since Latin and Folk, World, & Country were removed on 2026-10-21, task-0079, plus the group Others), `genres` (475 entries: lowercase genre name to its `parents`, a list of families; `parents[0]` is the family Discogs files it under, further entries are other families it is also filed under, GEN-039, GEN-057), `discogs_names` (Discogs's own spelling where it differs from the stored one beyond case, spaces, hyphens and "&"), `unverified` (0 names kept without a successful search check, see below). Terminology (ADR-0030): every name below a family is a genre; a Discogs genre (Discogs calls it a style) is one of ours. The file was `genre_discogs.json` until 2026-10-19.
- `genre_extras.json`: `extras` (63 real genres Discogs lacks), each with `parents` (a list of family names or canonical names; `parents[0]` is the primary parent and gives the family, GEN-057), an optional `family` (override of the derived family: space disco, a level 3 genre under disco), and `note` (where it came from). Reviewed in git.
- `genre_levels.json` (not part of the loaded vocabulary files above, validated by `genre_vocab.load_levels`): the three-level tree, see [genre-levels.md](../../docs/tech/genre-levels.md): `parents` per genre instead of the old `parent`, `also_under` and `also_in_genres`; 20 genres still list a second family (14 of them carry `fusion: true`, GEN-061, GEN-067); 33 genres carry `related` (22 moved associations, 11 Rock, GEN-067) (reviewed associations moved out of `parents`: a list of genre keys or family names, no effect on the tree, levels, families, counts or colours, GEN-066); `display` is lowercase for genres (drum & bass, psy-trance; only families keep capitals, GEN-065) and is absent when it equals the key; `sovietwave` is one word like synthwave; Electronic genres carry an optional `subfamily` (one of the eight names declared in the top-level `subfamilies` block, GEN-068; level 3 genres inherit it through `parents[0]`; provisional placements: hardcore and its children in Techno, edm in House, kuduro in Breaks & bass), the per-family `_meta` counts stay by primary family (total 530, Electronic 145, Rock 111: 42 level 2, 69 level 3; `_meta.drafted` lists Electronic and Rock, the 20 held Rock genres keep `provisional: true`).
- `genre_families.json` is gone: the vocabulary replaces it (GEN-038 step 1).

Stored spelling: Discogs's name lowercased, except where the project already used another spelling (drum and bass, rock and roll, rhythm and blues, psytrance, synthpop, hi-nrg, hip house, euro disco, dance pop and similar). Lookups ignore case, spaces, hyphens, accents and "&" versus "and". Discogs genres that TAG-073 drops as junk (comedy, novelty, parody) or TAG-075 retags (romantic) are left out.

### Source and terms (TAG-084)

- Source: the Genres and Styles list of the Discogs database guidelines (Discogs help centre). Only names and the genre to family relation are stored; no release data, no runtime dependency.
- Terms: Discogs publishes its monthly data dumps into the public domain (CC0) and its API terms allow non-commercial use with an identifying User-Agent; names of Discogs genres and styles are facts, not releases. The help centre and the terms pages answered with a bot check (HTTP 403) when this list was made, so the wording was not re-read then: confirm the terms once from a browser (open point of task-0035).
- How this list was made (2026-10-16): the help article could not be fetched, so the list was written from memory of it, extended with candidate names from the earlier reviewed table and the live library, and every name was then checked against the public Discogs database search (`https://api.discogs.com/database/search?style=<name>&per_page=10`, no token, one request every 2.5 s, under the 25 per minute limit, User-Agent `ElectrophoneVocabCheck/1.0`). A name is kept when releases with exactly that Discogs style exist (840 names were checked; 307 candidates with no exact match were left out). The result had 512 genres (476 after the removed families of 2026-10-18), fewer than the roughly 550 of the Discogs list: names that could not be recalled are missing, and the diff against the article (open point of task-0035) closes the gap; until then a missing genre can be added to `genre_extras.json`. The search cannot tell which genre a style belongs to (genre and style filter independently), so the style to genre relation comes from the reviewed table of ADR-0021 and from memory of the list; `also_under` is advisory. Names without a check are listed in `unverified` (0).

### Refresh

1. Get the current list (the help article in a browser, or single checks with the search above).
2. Diff it against `genre_discogs.json` (new, renamed and removed names); keep the stored spelling rules above.
3. Review in git; an extras entry that becomes a Discogs genre moves out of `genre_extras.json`.
4. `sh scripts/check.sh` (the validator tests of TAG-083 run on the new files); deploy; read `backups-genre-merge/review-<uid>.json` after the first start.

### Review notes

- Library tags that no rule maps (kept on the songs, listed in the review file): aaron smith, joris, medley, music video, party, popcorn, skrux, ⚈⚈.
- ALIASES and RETAGS targets must be canonical; add an alias in `app/genre_filter.py` before adding an extras entry for a mere spelling variant.

## Genre sources (GEN-066, ADR-0033)

`genre_sources.json` records, for every key of the vocabulary (the 11 families and 529 genres), which external catalogues have the genre. Optional at run time: `app/genre_sources.py` loads it once at start; if it is missing or malformed the server runs without it (`sources` is `{}` on every row). `genre_sources_review.json` lists candidates that were not stored (genre, candidate, reason) for the owner to decide. Both are generated; do not edit them by hand.

- Shape: `{"<genre key>": {"discogs": true|false, "discogs_name": "...", "musicbrainz": {"id", "name", "match"}, "wikipedia": {"title", "wikidata", "match"}, "apple_music": {"id", "name", "match"}}, "_meta": {...}}`. Only sources that matched are present; absent means not found. No URLs are stored.
- `discogs` is the `discogs` flag of `genre_levels.json` (true for the 11 families); `discogs_name` is Discogs's own spelling when it differs from the key.
- Spotify is not stored: it has no official genre catalogue.
- Rebuild (WSL, Node): `node scripts/build-genre-sources.mjs [--cache DIR]`. Raw downloads are cached in DIR (default `$GENRE_SOURCES_CACHE`, else the OS temp dir; never in the repo), at most one network request per 1.1 s, User-Agent `ElectrophoneGenreSources/1.0`. Delete the cache or pass `--refresh` to refetch. Sources: MusicBrainz `genre/all` (paged), the iTunes genre list `itunes.apple.com/WebObjects/MZStoreServices.woa/ws/genres` (Music subtree), Wikidata `wbsearchentities` and `wbgetentities` (English Wikipedia titles come from the sitelinks).
- Match rules (`match`): `name` = the normalised genre key equals the normalised catalogue name; `synonym` = a normalised synonym or display name from `genre_levels.json` equals it (only when no name match); `manual` = an entry of an optional `genre_sources_manual.json` (`{"<genre>": {"<source>": {...same fields...}}}`), which also removes that genre's review rows. Normalisation: lowercase, accents folded, "&" read as "and", hyphens, spaces and punctuation ignored, a trailing "music" word dropped. Nothing fuzzy.
- Review list: several candidates for one genre, a catalogue entry claimed by two genres (the one with a name match wins; otherwise both are reviewed), Wikipedia articles whose Wikidata item is not a music genre. Apple Music repeats names in its Classical tree (for example Electronic, Opera, Latin Jazz): the shallowest entry wins when it is unique, otherwise the genre is reviewed. Wikipedia matches the Wikidata label first, then an alias, and only items that are a music genre (Q188451) with an English article.
- Links: `GET /genres/vocabulary` rows carry `sources` (the stored ids, plus `discogs: {name, family}`) and `source_links`, `[{source, url, kind}]` built by `genre_source_links` in `app/genre_sources.py` (the only place that builds URLs; the web has no URL logic). Order: wikipedia, apple_music, spotify, discogs, rym, musicbrainz. Rate Your Music: only the curated URL in `genre_rym.json` (`{"<genre>": "https://rateyourmusic.com/..."}`, empty for now, Alex supplies the URLs), kind page; none for a genre without an entry. Rules: Wikipedia `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<title>` (page) or, unmatched, `https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=<key> music genre` (search); Apple Music always `https://music.apple.com/us/search?term=<matched Apple name or key>` (search; Apple's genre pages `.../genre/id<id>` redirect to the search page, checked 2026-10-19); Spotify always `https://open.spotify.com/search/genre%3A%22<key>%22` (search); Discogs always: `https://www.discogs.com/search/?style=<name>&type=release` for genres in Discogs (`genre=` for a family), else `https://www.discogs.com/search/?q=<key>&type=release` (search; Discogs answers bots with 403, so the URL scheme is not verified); MusicBrainz `https://musicbrainz.org/genre/<id>` (page) only when matched. Names are percent-encoded (space `%20`, `&` `%26`, `/` `%2F`).

### Hierarchy evidence (review only, DQ1)

`genre_hierarchy_evidence.json` holds, for each genre with a Wikipedia match in `genre_sources.json`, where Wikipedia and Wikidata put it in a hierarchy: `wikidata`, `wikipedia`, `subclass_of` and `instance_of` (Wikidata P279 and P31 with `q`, English `label`, vocabulary `key` or null), `lead_parents` (links of the first sentence after "subgenre of", "genre/style/form of", "fusion of", "offshoot/derivative of", "derived/evolved/emerged from", with `phrase_kind` subgenre, genre_of, fusion or offshoot), `origins` and `derivatives` (links of the infobox fields `stylistic_origins` and `derivatives`). A label maps to a vocabulary key through the Wikipedia title, then the name, then a synonym; unknown or ambiguous labels stay listed with `key: null`. It is NOT loaded by the server and not in the payload; it feeds decisions only.

- Rebuild (WSL, after `build-genre-sources.mjs`): `node scripts/build-genre-hierarchy.mjs [--cache DIR]` (cached and resumable, one request per 1.1 s; Wikipedia lead and infobox come from `action=parse&prop=wikitext&section=0`, Wikidata from `wbgetentities`).
- Compare with the working-tree `genre_levels.json`: `node scripts/hierarchy-diff.mjs [--out DIR] [--family NAME]` writes `hierarchy-diff-<family>.md` per family and prints counts. Additions are evidence parents missing from `parents[]` (ancestors already in our tree are skipped): STRONG = Wikidata and the lead agree, MEDIUM = one of them, WEAK = infobox origins only. Removals are our parents named by no evidence (and not an ancestor of an evidence parent), graded by how many of Wikidata and the lead had parents at all. Contradictions are evidence parents that are our descendants.
- Tests: `scripts/genre-hierarchy.test.mjs` (parser, resolver, diff), `server/tests/test_genre_sources.py` (file keys).
