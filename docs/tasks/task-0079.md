# Remove the Latin and Folk, World, & Country families

> Alex: kill Latin and Folk, World, & Country as level 1 nodes; their genres must not be orphaned.

Decided 2026-10-21 (chat). Both families leave level 1. Their direct children go under the temporary group Others; a genre that has another parent keeps only that parent.

| | Latin | Folk, World, & Country |
|---|---|---|
| Direct children | 58 | 69 |
| Re-parented under Others | 56 | 65 |
| Keep the other parent only | 2 (bossa nova, latin jazz: Jazz) | 4 (country rock, folk rock: Rock; folk metal: heavy metal; folk punk: punk) |

121 genres moved to Others (127 direct children in all, 6 kept their other parent); every genre stays in the vocabulary, `folk` among them (now an ordinary genre under Others). `latin` was never a genre: the node is dropped. The six genres with another parent lost their `fusion` flag (a fusion genre needs two families).

- Old family names resolve as synonyms of Others (latin, latin music, latino, folk, world, & country and its spellings, world music, folk and country, world and country): no conflict with any vocabulary key, display name or synonym.
- Songs tagged `genre:latin` or `genre:folk, world, & country` (families used to be assignable) are not changed: the value is no longer a vocabulary name, so the song keeps it as an unmappable tag (TAG-082) and `POST` with it is rejected as a group name. No migration was added; add the two names to `server/data/retired_genres.json` if Alex wants them stripped.
- Legacy `GET /genres/graph` (Mac app): `FAMILIES` is now 9 names; descendants fold to the remaining legacy families by the existing rule, Others to Electronic.
- Web: `FAMILIES` and `FAMILY_ORDER` in `web/js/genre-data.js` lose the two names; palette slots 8 (Latin) and 10 (Folk) are free, so `legacySlot` uses an explicit table and `SPARE_SLOTS` gets both.

Built, not deployed, tests not run.
