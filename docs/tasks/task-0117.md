## Physics

- Sound comes from the solid body of the instrument itself.
- A free bar (xylophone, marimba, vibraphone) has partials at about 1, 2.76, 5.40 and 8.93 times the base frequency.
- Bells and cymbals have many inharmonic partials.

## Controls to build

- Material and size (they set the base frequency and the decay).
- Strike (position and hardness).
- Decay per partial.
- Tuned-bar ratios (marimba and vibraphone bars are shaped so that the second partial sits at 4 or 3 times the base instead of 2.76).

## Open questions (recommendations, not decisions)

- A. Bells and cymbals: a fixed partial table per instrument or generated ratios? Recommended: fixed tables for a few named sounds (bar, tuned marimba bar, bell), no cymbal until the others are heard.
- B. How are material and size mapped? Recommended: one "base frequency" control plus a "material" choice that sets the decay times; no physical units on screen.
- C. How many partials? Recommended: up to 8 for bars, up to 16 for bells.
- D. Strike hardness as a roll-off of the partial amplitudes? Recommended: yes, one dB-per-octave control like the lab's decay across harmonics.
