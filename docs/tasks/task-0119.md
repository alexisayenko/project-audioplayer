> Alex (decision HU3, 2026-10-25): music theory goes into the Glossary now, as a sixth map domain "Music theory" with its core terms; a separate Theory page with examples you can play is a ticket, not built yet.

Follow-up of [task-0114](task-0114.md) (the site, ADR-0049, its Glossary map) and [task-0109](task-0109.md) (harmonics lab extras); site: [studio-site.md](../specs/studio-site.md), [ADR-0049](../tech/decisions.md). Nothing is built: the Glossary has the terms (Scale, Major scale, Minor scale, Mode, Key, Tonic, Semitone, Whole tone, Tuning, Equal temperament, Just intonation, Pythagorean tuning, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Triad, Chord, Root, Inversion, Seventh chord, Chord progression, Cadence, Consonance, Dissonance, Melody, Circle of fifths, Chromatic scale, Pentatonic scale, Reference pitch, plus Harmony, Octave and Interval moved into the domain) and 44 links between them; the Synthesize pages play single tones or one harmonic series.

## What the page shows

- Scales and modes: pick a tonic and a scale or mode, see the notes and the semitone and whole-tone steps, and play it up and down.
- Chords: pick a root and a chord type (major and minor triad, seventh chords), see its notes and inversions, and play it (together and as an arpeggio).
- Chord progressions: a short sequence such as I-IV-V-I in a chosen key, played in order, with the cadence marked.
- Intervals as ratios and cents: for each interval the just ratio (3:2, 5:4, 4:3), its size in cents just and equal-tempered, and a way to hear the two together; the beating of a slightly mistuned interval.
- Tuning: switch between equal temperament, just intonation and Pythagorean tuning and hear the same chord in each; reference pitch control (A4 = 440 Hz by default).
- Circle of fifths: a clickable diagram of the twelve keys; selecting one shows its scale and plays its chord.
- Every term on the page links to the Glossary entry.

## Open questions (recommendations, not decisions)

- A. Which voice plays the examples? Recommended: reuse the Chordophones synth voice (piano-like, with the harmonic series and decay of the lab), so a chord sounds like an instrument and the beating between tunings is audible; a plain sine as a "pure" option.
- B. How are notes scheduled? Recommended: Web Audio scheduling on the audio clock (start times computed from `AudioContext.currentTime`, a short look-ahead), not `setTimeout` timing, so arpeggios and progressions keep time.
- C. Where does it live in the menu? Recommended: a new item "Theory" in the Reference group beside the Glossary, since it explains terms; the Synthesize group stays for sound sources.
- D. One page or sections? Recommended: one page with five cards (Scales and modes, Chords, Progressions, Intervals and tuning, Circle of fifths), each usable alone, built in that order.
- E. Notation? Recommended: note names and a piano keyboard diagram first (no staff notation); a staff can be a later ticket.
- F. Note names for sharps and flats. Recommended: show sharps and flats by key (C major with no accidentals, F major with B-flat) and keep the Glossary's ASCII spelling (E-flat) in text.
- G. Loudness safety: chords sum several voices. Recommended: scale the voice gain by the number of notes and use the same limiter as the other synth pages.
