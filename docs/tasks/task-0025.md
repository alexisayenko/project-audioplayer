# Audio equalizer (v2)

Future and deferred: nothing is decided or scheduled. "v2" means after the visualizer phases 1 and 2 ([task-0023](task-0023.md)), which create the shared audio graph. Details marked **(proposed)** are suggestions; see [Open questions](#open-questions-for-alex). Web first; the iOS app is a separate change.

## Two meanings of "equalizer"

1. **Audible equalizer:** adjust the level of frequency bands in the sound you hear. This task.
2. **Visual spectrum bars** that dance to the music. Already phase 1 of [task-0023](task-0023.md). Out of scope here.

Technically not hard: the Web Audio API has `BiquadFilterNode`; ten of them in a chain are a standard graphic EQ. The work is the shared graph order, the UI, persistence and the iOS/Safari quirks.

## Design

### Graph order (proposed)

```text
media element source -> preamp GainNode -> [10 BiquadFilterNode chain] -> (optional limiter)
  -> normalisation GainNode (task-0020) -> AnalyserNode (task-0023) -> destination
```

- Built lazily in the single shared graph, `web/js/audio-graph.js` (today: source -> gain -> analyser -> destination). The EQ chain is inserted between the source and the normalisation gain.
- **Analyser after the EQ (proposed, question E):** the bars show what you hear, including the boosted bands. The alternative (before) shows the raw song.
- Normalisation gain ([task-0020](task-0020.md)) applies after the EQ and preamp, so it sees the final level.
- `createMediaElementSource` is irreversible per element; the graph, once created, stays for the session. Bypass therefore flattens the EQ in place (below), it does not remove nodes.

### Bands (proposed)

10-band graphic EQ, centres in Hz and filter type:

| Band | 31 | 62 | 125 | 250 | 500 | 1k | 2k | 4k | 8k | 16k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type | lowshelf | peaking | peaking | peaking | peaking | peaking | peaking | peaking | peaking | highshelf |

- Peaking bands: Q about 1.41 (one-octave width, adjacent bands overlap smoothly).
- Gain per band -12..+12 dB, step 0.5. 0 dB = flat.
- Frequency resolution is not a limit: a biquad's centre frequency is an arbitrary parameter and its width is set by Q. The analyser is different: FFT bin width = sampleRate / fftSize (44.1 kHz: fftSize 2048 gives about 21.5 Hz per bin; 32768 gives about 1.35 Hz per bin with a window of about 0.74 s). The visualizer shows about 128 log-spaced bars, so it does not need fine bins either. A 1 Hz-wide band is pointless musically.

### Preamp and headroom (proposed)

- Preamp `GainNode` in front of the chain, default automatic headroom = -(largest positive band gain) dB, so a +6 dB boost cannot clip; a manual override is allowed (phase 2).
- Optional soft limiter after the chain: `DynamicsCompressorNode`, threshold -1 dB, ratio 20, knee 0 (question B). More boost than the headroom compensates, or none, is the user's choice.

### Bypass and A/B

- Bypass switch keeps the chain connected and ramps all band gains to 0 dB and the preamp to 0 dB with `setTargetAtTime` (time constant about 10 ms, settled in about 30 ms), no click, no reconnecting.
- A/B compare: two stored settings slots; switching ramps the same way. **(proposed)** simple "Compare with flat" via bypass in phase 1, A/B slots later.

### Presets (proposed, question A)

Flat, Bass boost, Treble boost, Vocal, Rock, Electronic/Dance, Acoustic, Loudness, Late night. Exact gain tables are chosen by ear during phase 1 and live in one pure module. A manual change after a preset shows "Custom".

### Settings model (platform neutral)

JSON: `{ "version": 1, "enabled": true, "preamp": null | dB, "bands": [{"f": 31, "type": "lowshelf", "gain": 0}, ...], "preset": "Flat" | "Custom" | name }`. Band list plus gains only, so iOS can map it onto `AVAudioUnitEQ` bands unchanged.

### Persistence

- Per device, `localStorage` via `web/js/storage.js` (every read and write in try/catch; bad stored values fall back to Flat).
- Optional per-user sync later: settings stored with the profile (question C); needs a server field and is not part of phase 1. Server involvement is otherwise none; the EQ is client-only.

### UI (proposed)

- Playback screen: an "Equalizer" button opens a floating panel (closes on Escape, outside click optional).
- Panel: 10 vertical sliders with `role="slider"`, `aria-valuemin/max`, `aria-valuetext` such as "250 Hz, plus 3.5 dB"; arrows step 0.5 dB (PageUp/PageDown 3 dB, Home/End min and max); Reset (all 0 dB); preset menu; on/off switch; "Compare with flat" toggle.
- Phone layout: panel is a bottom sheet, sliders at least 44 px touch width, labels under each slider.
- Guest mode: allowed, local only (no server write).
- Reduced motion: no animation needed.
- Live spectrum overlay behind the sliders reusing the analyser (phase 2); drawn only while the panel is open.

## Platform notes

- **iOS Safari:** the graph starts on a user gesture (the first Play or the first EQ switch); `createMediaElementSource` is irreversible; the `AudioContext` can be suspended on the lock screen and playback must keep working (same fallback and timeout as `audio-graph.js` `enable`, state `blocked` or `failed` leaves plain media-element playback and the EQ control disabled with a hint).
- **CPU:** 10 biquads plus one compressor is negligible; stay inside the [task-0023](task-0023.md) budget (below 8% on a mid-range phone, with the visualizer off).
- **Latency:** none worth noting; the chain adds no buffering.
- **Tempo** ([task-0024](task-0024.md)): unaffected, the EQ sees the already stretched sound.
- **iOS native parity:** `AVAudioUnitEQ` with a band array in `AVAudioEngine`; separate change in `ios/Sources`, same settings JSON.

## Later phases

- **31-band (third-octave) graphic EQ:** more precision, much busier UI (question D).
- **Parametric EQ:** 3-5 bands with frequency, gain and Q.

## Phases and acceptance

### Phase 1: 10-band graphic EQ, presets, bypass, persistence

- [ ] Shared graph (`audio-graph.js`) gets the preamp and 10-filter chain; with all gains 0 and the EQ off the sound is bit-for-bit as before to the ear (no level change, no click).
- [ ] Band types and Q as in the table; gain clamped -12..+12, step 0.5.
- [ ] Presets apply, show "Custom" after a manual change; Reset restores Flat.
- [ ] Bypass and "Compare with flat" switch without a click (gain ramp).
- [ ] Settings persist per device, with a safe fallback on corrupt or missing storage.
- [ ] Panel is keyboard operable and screen-reader readable (`aria-valuetext` in dB); works on a phone.
- [ ] Works in guest mode (local only).
- [ ] iOS Safari: playback and lock-screen controls keep working when the graph is blocked, suspended or fails.
- [ ] Normalisation ([task-0020](task-0020.md)) and the visualizer ([task-0023](task-0023.md)) still work with the new chain.

### Phase 2: preamp, limiter, spectrum overlay

- [ ] Automatic headroom and manual preamp; optional limiter (if question B says yes); live spectrum behind the sliders.

## Tests

- Pure helpers (`web/js/eq.test.mjs`): preset tables (10 values each, within -12..+12), headroom (-max positive gain, 0 for all-negative), dB to linear gain, clamp and step rounding, `aria-valuetext` strings, serialise/parse of the settings JSON (version, missing fields, NaN, wrong types fall back to Flat).
- Graph: fake `AudioContext` and `BiquadFilterNode` in the DOM shim; the node order, filter types, frequencies, Q, gain and bypass ramp calls.
- DOM: sliders (click, arrows, PageUp/PageDown, Home/End), preset menu, Reset, persistence through `storage.js` including a throwing storage.
- Manual listening checklist on a phone and a laptop: no click on bypass or preset change, no audible distortion at +12 dB on one band with headroom on, lock-screen playback continues, bars match the heard sound.

## Risks

- **Distortion:** large boosts clip without headroom or a limiter.
- **iOS audio session:** a suspended or blocked `AudioContext` must not stop playback.
- **No removal:** the graph cannot be undone once `createMediaElementSource` ran; bypass is a flat chain, not a disconnect.
- **UI complexity:** ten sliders on a phone, plus presets, plus compare.
- **Preset taste:** gain tables are subjective; keep the list short and editable by changing one module.
