# On-air volume normalisation: keep the output at a chosen level

> Alex: volume on-air normalization: I set the desired level of output in dB and the volume is automatically adjusted continuously to match my expectation, plus or minus some percent.

## How it differs from task-0020

[task-0020](task-0020.md) normalises **per song, once**: it measures a song's loudness and applies one fixed gain, so every song starts at about the same level. This task works **during playback, continuously**: it watches the signal that is actually coming out and moves a gain up or down so the output stays near the level the owner chose, even inside one song (quiet intro, loud chorus, a podcast-like voice) and across songs with different masters.

## Proposed behaviour

- A Settings control "Target level" in dB (for example -23 to -10 LUFS, a slider with a numeric value and an Off position; default Off) and a "Tolerance" (plus or minus a few dB or a percent of the target, default about +-2 dB). The range limits, the units and the label are decided when built.
- The output loudness is measured continuously (short-term loudness, about 3 s window, ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128 style K-weighting; a cheaper RMS or peak meter is the fallback). A gain stage moves toward the target only when the measured level leaves the tolerance band, with slow attack and slower release, so the music does not "breathe" and short dynamics (a snare hit) are left alone.
- A **limiter** after the gain stage prevents clipping when the gain goes up; the maximum boost is capped (for example +12 dB) so a silent passage is not blown up into noise; the gain freezes during silence and during a pause.
- Works on top of the volume slider: the slider stays the master volume, the normaliser keeps the level near the target at whatever the slider setting means in dB. Combined with task-0020 the static per-song gain is applied first and the on-air stage corrects only the remainder.
- Visual feedback inline (WEB-115): a small level meter or a "Gain +3.2 dB" note on the dock volume control while it works, no toast.
- Per device: web and iOS store their own settings.

## Web design sketch

Web Audio graph (extends task-0023 and task-0025): `<audio>` -> `MediaElementSource` -> per-song `GainNode` (task-0020) -> **normaliser** (an `AudioWorklet` computing short-term loudness and an `AudioParam`-driven gain, or a `DynamicsCompressorNode` as a first version) -> limiter -> `AnalyserNode` (visualiser) -> destination. If the audio context is suspended or blocked, playback keeps working without normalisation (playback reliability comes first).

## iOS

AVPlayer with an audio tap or `AVAudioEngine` and a dynamics processor; background and lock-screen playback must keep working and is verified on a device; needs Alex's permission to edit `ios/`.

## Open questions

- A. Algorithm: a true loudness measurement (LUFS, K-weighting) with a slow gain follower, or a compressor plus a limiter (simpler, changes dynamics more)? Recommended: start with the gain follower, because it keeps the music's dynamics and honours the "target level" idea.
- B. Unit: dB (LUFS) as asked, with the percent only as the tolerance. A dBFS-relative reading of the slider is confusing, so the target is a loudness, not a slider position.
- C. Interplay with the dock volume slider: master volume on top, or does the normaliser replace the slider's effect? Recommended: the slider stays the master, the target is the "what I want to hear at slider 100%".
- D. Anything that runs only while the screen is on: on iOS background playback, does the audio tap keep running? To verify on a device before promising it.
- E. Latency, battery and CPU on the phone with a worklet running all the time.

## Phases

1. Web: the meter and the gain follower in an `AudioWorklet`, the limiter, the Settings control, tests on synthetic signals (a step in level, silence, a loud transient), and a manual listening test.
2. Web: combine with the per-song gain of task-0020 and the visualiser tap.
3. iOS (later, with permission).

## Acceptance (to refine when built)

- A synthetic test tone that jumps by 12 dB settles inside the tolerance band within a few seconds and does not overshoot the limiter ceiling; silence leaves the gain unchanged; a pause freezes it; Off restores unity gain and bit-exact output.
- Spec ids in docs/specs/playback.md (setting, behaviour, limits) and web.md; playback never fails when the normaliser is unavailable.

## Not in scope

- Equaliser, bass boost and any other tone control.
