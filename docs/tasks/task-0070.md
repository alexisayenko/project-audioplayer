# Named feature space: a map of songs and genres along measurable dimensions

> Alex: "should we have something like this for genres?" (his colour-ontology project, https://color.isayenko.net/?view=oklch, a 3D map of colours in a perceptual space) and "music has bpm, instruments, rhythmical patterns". Create a new task that describes this "named feature space".

## Idea

Colour categories are prototype categories: everyone agrees on the centre of green, and the border to blue is a soft gradient (prototype theory; Berlin and Kay; Russian has two basic blues). Genres behave the same way: a clear centre (Kraftwerk is electronic, Metallica is heavy metal) and soft, culture-dependent borders. A colour has a physical space under it (OKLCH: lightness, chroma, hue). Music has measurable dimensions too, so a genre can be shown as a **region** in a space, not as a box in a tree: a **soft cloud around its prototype songs**, with fusion songs in the overlaps.

This task describes that space and the views on it. It extends [task-0064](task-0064.md) (distance and similarity: tag-based first, audio embeddings later) with a **readable, named** space beside the hidden learned one.

## The dimensions (decided with Alex, 2026-10-20)

| Dimension | Defines a genre? | What it is | How to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rhythm pattern** | Yes, a core dimension | The drum and onset pattern: four-on-the-floor (house), breakbeat (drum and bass), backbeat (rock), swing (jazz), syncopation, clave | Onset detection, tempogram, beat histogram, a small classifier for named patterns |
| **Instrumentation and timbre** | Yes, a core dimension | Which instruments carry the music and their "sound colour": distorted guitar (metal), strings (classical), analog saw synths (synthwave), acoustic against electronic. Timbre is merged here: it is how an instrument sounds | Instrument classifiers (for example Essentia models), embedding probes, spectral features (brightness, roughness, bass weight) |
| **Harmony** | For some genres | The chords and scales used and how they move: blues has its 12-bar pattern and blue notes, jazz extended chords, techno often a single repeating chord, pop a few loop chords | Chroma, key and mode, chord recognition |
| **Tempo (BPM)** | Conditional | Defining inside dance genres (house about 120-130, techno about 125-150, drum and bass about 160-180); weak for rock, jazz and pop, so it is used only where a genre family has a typical range | Already stored per song (`bpm`) |
| Vocals | No | Not defining for a genre; at most a modifier or microgenre feature (vocal trance) | Not a dimension; keep as a modifier |
| Loudness and dynamics | No | Follows the mastering era, not the genre | Not a dimension (task-0020 handles level) |
| Era | No | A point in history: lineage and origin metadata (`releaseYear`, the Tradition axis), not a feature | Metadata only |

Relation to the model of ADR-0038: instrumentation and timbre are the **medium** axis, rhythm and harmony are part of the **form** axis, tempo and energy relate to the **purpose** axis (dance). The tree stays the human-made structure; the space is the measured one, and the two can be compared (which genres are sound-coherent, which are not).

## Views

1. **Genre space map:** every song is a point (2D first, 3D later), coloured by its family; a genre is shown as a soft density cloud around the centroid of its songs; its **prototype songs** (the nearest to the centroid) are marked; hovering a song lists its nearest neighbours and its distance to the centre of each genre it carries.
2. **Song card:** "Nearest genres by sound" (the three closest genre centres) next to the tagged genres; a gentle hint where the two disagree (the tag-outlier check of task-0064).
3. **Axes sliders (later):** pick two or three named dimensions as the axes of the map (rhythm against instrumentation, for example), the way OKLCH lets one pick lightness, chroma and hue.

4. **Prototypes (`prototypes` field):** each genre can store its reference (etalon) songs as a list in the genre data: song title with artist and, when the song is in the library, its song id. They are shown on the genre infobox and used as the anchors (prototype songs) of the map, instead of only the songs nearest to the centroid. See the [etalon test](../tech/genre-terminology.md#the-etalon-test).

## Phases

0. **Prototypes, stored by hand** for the level-1 forms, starting with six examples: house "Your Love", techno "No UFOs", trance "For an Angel", drum and bass "Brown Paper Bag", synthwave "Nightcall", ambient "Music for Airports".
1. **Tag and metadata space (no audio):** song vectors from genres (weighted by tree distance), BPM (only inside dance families) and year (only for the era filter); a 2D projection (PCA or UMAP) computed offline and served as a static JSON; the map view on the web. Reuses phase 1 of task-0064.
2. **Rhythm and tempo features:** extract onset-based rhythm descriptors and a pattern label per downloaded song as an offline job; add as dimensions.
3. **Instrumentation and timbre features:** instrument classifiers or embeddings with named probes (guitar, synth, drums, strings, acoustic or electronic); add as dimensions.
4. **Harmony features:** key, mode and a chord-movement summary; used only where it separates genres (blues, jazz, classical).
5. **Learned embeddings (hidden space)** as in task-0064 phase 2, used as an independent second opinion and to validate that the named dimensions explain the clusters.

## Open questions

- A. Which pattern labels does the rhythm classifier know (four-on-the-floor, breakbeat, backbeat, swing, half-time, clave...) and where do the training examples come from?
- B. A library of about 420 songs is small: prototypes and clouds are fine for the view, but statistics (are these dimensions separating genres?) need a larger reference set; use public datasets only for validation?
- C. How are conditional dimensions (tempo, harmony) weighted in a distance, and how do we show that a dimension does not apply to a genre?
- D. Where do features live: a small vector file per song next to the cover (recomputed only when the audio changes), never in the playback path.
- E. Projection stability: new songs should not move the whole map (anchor points or incremental projection); the prototypes are natural anchors.
- F. Prototypes: who picks them (the owner), how many per genre (3), and what if a prototype is not in the library (a link to the Wikipedia article of the song).

## Acceptance (to refine when built)

- Phase 1: spec ids in docs/specs/genres.md (space map) and library.md (nearest genres on the card); a test that the projection is deterministic for a fixed library and that a prototype song of each genre lies nearer its own centre than another genre's.
- Feature extraction runs as an offline background job; playback and offline reliability are untouched.

## Not in scope

- Replacing the genre tree: the tree stays the human structure; the space only measures and checks it.
- Recommendations from other users' data and any sharing of features.
